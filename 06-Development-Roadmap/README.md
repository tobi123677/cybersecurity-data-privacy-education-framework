# K–12 Cybersecurity and Data Privacy Framework — Development Roadmap

## Overview

This roadmap outlines the development progress, planned improvements, and future evaluation activities for the K–12 Cybersecurity and Data Privacy Education Framework.

The initiative aims to develop practical cybersecurity education, self-assessment, and readiness resources for K–12 students, educators, and school personnel.

The roadmap distinguishes existing research and prototype work from activities that are planned but have not yet been completed.

## Phase 1: Initial Research and Framework Design

**Status: Initial research completed; framework refinement ongoing**

Activities include:

- Developing the initial concept for the K–12 Cyber Safety Framework.
- Identifying key cybersecurity and data privacy topics relevant to K–12 education.
- Conducting a preliminary educator technology and cybersecurity training survey.
- Receiving 30 responses from 50 educators contacted, representing a 60% response rate.
- Preparing exploratory survey analysis using R Markdown.
- Identifying potential areas for further research and educational resource development.

The survey findings are preliminary and are not intended to represent all K–12 educators.

## Phase 2: Prototype and Resource Development

**Status: In progress**

Activities include:

- Designing initial Tech Check user interfaces using Figma.
- Developing a working Tech Check web prototype using Bubble.
- Exploring separate student and educator assessment pathways.
- Implementing and refining adaptive question-routing concepts.
- Developing the structure of a practical Cyber Safety Checklist.
- Creating a demonstration cybersecurity-readiness dashboard using synthetic data.

These resources remain under development and require additional testing before operational use.

## Phase 3: Testing and Refinement

**Status: Planned**

Planned activities include:

- Expanding and reviewing cybersecurity assessment question banks.
- Testing the accuracy and consistency of adaptive question routing.
- Refining assessment scoring and feedback methods.
- Conducting usability and accessibility testing.
- Gathering feedback from educators and relevant technical reviewers.
- Revising checklist content and framework guidance based on feedback.

## Phase 4: Pilot Evaluation

**Status: Planned; no institutional pilot confirmed**

Potential activities include:

- Seeking collaboration with interested K–12 educators or institutions.
- Establishing appropriate permissions and privacy safeguards.
- Conducting a limited pilot evaluation, if suitable partners and approvals are obtained.
- Evaluating usability, training relevance, and assessment performance.
- Identifying limitations and opportunities for improvement.

Any future pilot involving student information would require appropriate institutional authorization and data protection procedures.

## Phase 5: Expansion and Long-Term Development

**Status: Long-term objectives**

Potential future activities include:

- Refining the framework based on research and pilot findings.
- Developing additional cybersecurity training materials.
- Exploring scalable approaches to educator and student cybersecurity awareness.
- Improving readiness reporting and assessment accessibility.
- Investigating opportunities for broader educational collaboration and implementation.

These activities represent proposed future directions rather than completed outcomes or guaranteed partnerships.

## Current Project Evidence

The repository documents the following existing work:

- Preliminary educator survey and exploratory analysis.
- Initial K–12 Cyber Safety Framework documentation.
- Cyber Safety Checklist development documentation.
- Tech Check Figma interface designs and Bubble web prototype.
- Demonstration cybersecurity-readiness dashboard.

## Development and Evaluation Principles

The initiative will emphasize:

- Responsible handling of educational and personal information.
- Clear separation of synthetic demonstration data from actual research findings.
- Accessibility and practical relevance for K–12 educational settings.
- Evidence-based refinement through future testing and feedback.
- Transparent reporting of project progress, limitations, and outcomes.

## Conclusion

The K–12 Cybersecurity and Data Privacy Education Framework is an ongoing development initiative combining preliminary educator research, cybersecurity education resources, assessment prototyping, and readiness visualization.

The next major priorities are to refine the existing tools, obtain relevant feedback, and evaluate their potential usefulness in K–12 educational environments.
